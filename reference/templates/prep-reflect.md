## How the Week Landed

One paragraph on the shape of the week — execution quality, strategic versus reactive balance — and what you did well, named with evidence. Name the behavior and what it produced. If nothing stands out, say so in one line.

## Where Time Went

The gap between where the week went and where it mattered. The `Gap` column carries the read. Do not add a paragraph restating the table.

| Where you spent it | Where it mattered | Gap |
|---|---|---|

## What You Need to Face

The single most important thing you got wrong, ducked, or would rather not hear. These are usually one item, not three — name it once, with an honest read on why it keeps happening. Two items at most, the top one first.

If nothing qualifies this week, write one line saying so. An empty section is a valid result.

## Org Signal

What you learned about the org this week that hasn't fully landed. Not a status update — what the signal means for how you lead, what's fragile, what's stronger than you thought.

## Progress Markers

The longitudinal arc across several weeks: what has genuinely improved, and whether you are growing into the Director/VP-level operator this role requires. Concrete behavior, not adjectives — "you changed the process rather than promising to be more careful", not "you showed good judgment".

If an item from "What You Need to Face" is recurring, note the recurrence in one clause. Do not restate the item.

## Next Week

Directional advice, not a to-do list. Three items at most, prioritized: where to put energy, what to stop, what conversation to stop putting off.

Close with one orienting line you can return to mid-week.
